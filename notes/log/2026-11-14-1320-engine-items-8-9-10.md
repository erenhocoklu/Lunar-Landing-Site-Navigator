## Item 8: time-span control (1/2/3/5 years + alert)
Full landing-window calendar: hourly Sun/Earth flags from sky table + mask, then for every touchdown hour a 13-day window
with sunlight %, Earth %, longest night (sliding max), Sun elevation at touchdown, and a 20-point keep-out filter.

| Spots | 1 y | 2 y | 3 y | 5 y |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.1 ms | 1.8 ms | 2.5 ms | 4.8 ms |
| 500 | 0.33 s | 0.64 s | 0.93 s | 1.6 s |
| 5,000 | 3.3 s | 6.2 s | 8.9 s | 18.0 s |
All MEASURED, single thread. 5,000 x 5 y with Node worker threads (stand-in for Web Workers): 4 workers 6.2 s, 8 workers 4.5 s
(MEASURED, incl. start-up). A 6-day window run measured 2.4 s for 500 x 5 y vs 1.6 s for 13 days: run-to-run noise is ~50%.

- **Estimate before running:** `t = spots x years x 0.7 ms / workers_effective` (0.7 ms per spot-year MEASURED single thread;
  workers_effective ~3 for 4 workers, ~4 for 8). **Alert above 3 s** (~4,300 spot-years single thread).
- Plausible: **yes**. Build: ~2 h (Web Worker wrapper + progress bar + estimate). Main risk: slower phones/laptops (2-4x, UNVERIFIED).
- Cheap win (not measured): keep the hourly flags per spot after the first run; slider changes then only redo the window
  pass, roughly half the cost.

## Item 9: precompute physics, compute the rest live
- Live limits + mission length + Sun elevation at touchdown + keep-out are part of the numbers above: **per site 1-5 ms,
  5,000 spots 3-18 s** (MEASURED). Keep-out distance to 20 points per spot is negligible inside that.
- Plausible: **yes**. Build: ~1-2 h on top of item 8. Main risk: the longest-night value in the benchmark is an upper bound
  (night runs not clipped at the window start); the real code must clip it. Cost is the same.
- **Does anything precomputed go stale?** (all UNVERIFIED, reasoning, not measured)
  - Terrain (DEM) and horizon masks: no. They are fixed geometry.
  - Sky table: computed from JPL's DE441 ephemeris. The Moon's orbit and libration predictions for 2027-2031 are good to well
    under 0.001 deg in direction. Horizons uses Earth orientation (EOP) only for observers on Earth; ours are the Earth's centre
    or the Moon, so EOP predictions do not matter.
  - Future leap seconds are unknown: timestamps could be off by ~1 s. The sky moves ~0.00015 deg per second at the Moon. Negligible.
  - DE441 (sky) vs DE421 (terrain frame): tiny frame offset, already in GOTCHAS. No recomputation needed.
